# Signature vs method — what to copy and what to never copy

A deck built from this skill kept coming back recognisable as adgod's own work: a client's watch belted
around a moka pot, a model reading a newspaper with the watch as the focal point. Both are his frames. The
skill was handing them over by name.

**Separate the two things this house style contains.**

| Method — copy all of it | Signature — copy none of it |
|---|---|
| The 12-block prompt anatomy | The specific scene |
| Camera bodies, lenses, aperture logic | The specific prop combination |
| Lighting rigs, shadow-side clauses | The surreal conceit itself |
| Fidelity locks, contact physics, negatives | The exact crop and gesture of a known frame |
| The four selection heuristics in `ideation.md` | Anything a follower of his would recognise |
| Word budget, numeric specificity | |

The method is craft — it is how commercial photography has always been specified, and it transfers to any
product. The signature is authorship. Reusing it does not make your work look as good as his; it makes your
work look like his, which is the one outcome that has no value to a client.

## The do-not-restage list

These are adgod's, they appear in `corpus.md` and `archetypes.md`, and they are **off limits as scenes**. If
a draft lands on one, it is a fail — restart the frame from the product, not from the list.

- **A watch belted around a moka pot** as if the bracelet were the pot's waist (Rolex).
- **A watch as the focal point over a held newspaper**, model's face cropped to nose-bridge and jaw (Rolex).
- **A goldfish** sharing the frame with a luxury object (Rolex).
- **Inverted stacked cups / cup-and-lid balance** as a plinth for the product (Dior, Rhode).
- **The crushed aluminium can** beside its pristine twin (Liquid Death).
- **A product standing in a sculpted field of its own formula**, peaked like landscape (L'Occitane, Laneige).
- **A jar suspended inside a string net bag** bulging with fruit.
- **A product wrapped once by a heavy chrome curb chain**, suspended on a blue gradient (Salt & Stone).
- **Product buried in a heap of its own contents** — crisps, cream, churned butter.
- **A large cropped retro object in the foreground** — rotary phone, alarm clock, turntable, typewriter —
  with the product beside it.

The list is not exhaustive and it is not the point. The test is the question, not the enumeration.

## The originality gate — run it before delivering any frame

> **Would somebody who follows adgod recognise this frame as one of his?**

If yes, it fails, however well written it is. Two follow-ups when unsure:

1. **Strip the product. Is what remains a scene he already made?** The moka pot with a watch belted round it
   is his composition whether the watch is a Rolex or an AP. Swapping the client does not make it yours.
2. **Could this frame only exist for this product?** A frame derived from the product's own material,
   benefit, buyer or affordance is inherently original, because no other product produces the same set. A
   frame borrowed from his archive could carry any product, which is exactly why it reads as borrowed.

## Where scenes should come from instead

In order:

1. **The product itself, through the four heuristics** (`ideation.md`) — set extruded from its material, the
   invisible benefit made physical, the genre that carries its positioning, the buyer drawn as objects. This
   is the generative path and it produces frames that are yours.
2. **The affordance** (`adaptation.md`) — the gesture no competing product can perform. For a pocket watch,
   the case lowered into a pocket with the lanyard over the seam. Nobody else's archive contains it, because
   nobody else's product does that.
3. **Non-adgod photography** (`web-sourcing.md`) — brand newsrooms, photographers' portfolios, awards
   archives. Someone else's craft, restaged for your product, is normal art direction. His archive is not.

## What the library is for, corrected

`library.md` is **not a scene menu**, and treating it as one is what produced the failure above. It contains
420 photographs adgod selected for his own campaigns, plus 68 of his finished outputs — and the catalogue's
`kind` field is imperfect, so some of his outputs sit in the `reference-photo` bucket. The moka-pot frame is
one of them.

Use the library for three things:

- **Craft.** How he lights, how tight he crops, which palettes he pairs, what props he trusts. Read the
  distribution in `library.md` and apply the logic, not the frame.
- **Genre calibration.** What documentary-street or french-pharmacy actually looks like when it is done well.
- **A negative index.** Before committing to a scene, search the library for it. **A strong match is a
  warning, not a find** — it means you have arrived at something he has already published.

That last inversion is the important one. The library search that used to say *transcribe this* now also
says *avoid this*, and the same command answers both questions.
