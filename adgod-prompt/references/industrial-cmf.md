# Industrial / CMF register

The Braun–Rams to Teenage-Engineering lineage: bead-blasted aluminium and matte polymer on a soft grey
seamless, near-shadowless overcast light, shallow or tilt-shift focus, and exactly one saturated accent —
usually safety orange. CMF is the discipline term the industry actually uses: Colour, Material, Finish.

Use for a product whose design *is* the story — hardware with deliberate materials, chamfers, knurls,
translucency. Do not use for a product with no CMF to show, or for a service brand.

## What changes from the standard adgod register

Only four blocks move. Blocks 1–5, 8, 11 and 12 are unchanged.

| Block | Change |
|---|---|
| 6 environment | Seamless soft grey — warm grey, cool light grey, pale blue-grey or dark warm grey. No horizon, usually no visible surface at all; objects float in a volume of light. |
| 7 lighting | One very large overcast-sky softbox overhead and slightly forward. Near-shadowless, wide slow speculars travelling across metal, gentle terminators on chamfers, only faint ambient occlusion. Never dramatic. |
| 9 palette | Neutral grey / brushed silver / off-white, plus **exactly one** saturated accent — safety orange is canonical — placed on a single control surface. The accent is a chromatic event, not a colour scheme. |
| 10 tail | Two options, see below. |

## The two tails — this is the real decision

Much of the reference imagery in this genre is **rendered, not photographed**. Perfect gradients, impossible
depth of field and shadowless float are render signatures. The standard adgod tail actively fights that look,
so pick deliberately:

**Photographic tail** — for anything that should read as a real shoot:
> `Capture using a Phase One XF IQ4 150MP medium-format camera with a Schneider Kreuznach 120mm LS Macro Blue
> Ring lens, ISO 50, f/11, focus stacking, cross-polarized professional studio lighting, 16-bit colour depth,
> ultra-high dynamic range, museum-quality commercial retouching, exceptional micro-contrast and native 8K
> output. The final image must be indistinguishable from a real professionally art-directed industrial-design
> photoshoot.`

**Render tail** — for the clay-soft, floating, impossible-DOF look:
> `Rendered as a physically based studio visualisation with accurate material response, true subsurface
> behaviour in the translucent parts, correct index of refraction on every transparent surface, soft global
> illumination, ray-traced contact shadows and ambient occlusion, subtle real-lens depth of field and a faint
> film grain, 16-bit colour, ultra-high dynamic range, 8K output. The final image must read as a flawless CMF
> design visualisation of the kind an industrial design studio produces for a launch, clean and physically
> believable rather than illustrative.`

**Scope note:** the render tail is not derived from the adgod corpus — no deck in it uses one. It was written
for this register. The photographic tail is corpus-standard.

## Material vocabulary

Name finishes precisely; this register lives or dies on them.

`bead-blasted anodised aluminium with visible fine grain` · `matte injection-moulded polymer with a slight
orange-peel texture` · `knurled metal with individually resolved teeth` · `translucent polycarbonate with
real depth to it` · `rubberised keys with a soft sheen` · `anisotropic streaking along brushed metal
following its grain direction` · `display glass rendering as a deep neutral black rather than a lit screen`

And the geometry vocabulary: `parting lines` · `radii and chamfers` · `countersunk fasteners` · `vent slots`
· `port cutouts` · `material transitions` · `colour blocking`.
