# Aliveness — why one AI model reads as a person and the next reads as a mannequin

Written 2026-09-18 from three decks added that day: @prompts.soul BAGGY SEASON Vol. 008 (8 prompts, no
images), LUXRY.AI × Higgsfield "Soft Hours, Golden Light" (7 prompts, 7 images) and FASHIONNN.AI Reversed
Image Prompts Set 2 (7 prompts, 7 images). Fourteen images to look at, twenty-two prompts to count.

Evidence grades as in `principles.md`: MEASURED / CORPUS / THEORY / OBSERVED.

## The finding

**Aliveness is not beauty and it is not realism. It is evidence that the body was doing something a
fraction of a second before the shutter.** A mannequin frame can have perfect skin, perfect light and
perfect clothes. What it lacks is a body caught between two states.

The three decks reach that by different routes, and the routes stack.

## The ten levers

### A1 · Name a phone, not a camera
`Street style phone snapshot of …` opens **8 of 8** soul prompts. Set2 names an iPhone in 3 of 7 and a Sony
or Canon in 4 of 7 — and in my read the three iPhone frames (S01, S03, S04) carry more of the candid quality
than the Sony ones, though S03 is also the deck's most static pose, so the lever is not sufficient alone.
CORPUS + OBSERVED.

### A2 · Ask for the sensor's failures
`slight phone camera distortion, mild grain` closes **8 of 8** soul prompts. Set2's technical block asks for
`slight overexposure on face`, `slight lens flare`, `slight warm tone`. A clean render has no defects, so
naming defects is the cheapest realism signal there is. This is P3 stated for figures. CORPUS.

### A3 · Flat available light, zero instruments
`Flat available night light` · `Flat overcast daylight` · `Flat harsh fluorescent light` · `Flat grey
daylight` — **7 of 8**, plus one `Mixed warm night light`, so **8 of 8 are ambient**. The words *softbox* and
*studio* appear **0 times in the whole deck**. The deck says it outright: *"Ask for a phone snapshot, not a
photoshoot. The flatness is the realism."* CORPUS.

This directly contradicts the adgod grammar, which is rigs and shadow sides. Both work. They are different
jobs: adgod lights an object, these decks catch a person.

### A4 · A named expression that is not a smile
**0 of 8** soul prompts contain the word *smile*. Instead: `neutral expression`, `mouth set flat`, `blank
expression`, `chin lifted` ×2, `chin raised`, `head lowered`, `head tilted slightly down`, `looking off to
the side`, `looking straight into the lens`. LUXRY S03 asks for `a dreamy, slightly bored daydream stare`.
A stated non-smile is a decision; an unstated expression defaults to the stock grin. CORPUS.

Note the exception that proves it: the two most alive Set2 frames are both **laughing**. A laugh works when
it is attached to an action (`laughing joyfully with head thrown back`). A smile floating on a still body is
what reads as stock.

### A5 · One hand, doing one specific thing
The soul deck runs a single gesture through all eight frames — a hand at the cap — and varies it every time:
`both hands raised to the brim`, `tugging the brim down over her eyes`, `one hand adjusting`, `tipping his
cap forward`, `both hands pulling the brim low`. The deck's own note: *"One hand on the cap. It is the pose
that makes the whole set read as one."* CORPUS.

This is also a deck-consistency device, not only an aliveness device — a repeated gesture binds a set the way
a repeated palette does.

### A6 · Body torsion — face and torso pointing different ways
Every frame I read as alive has it: over-the-shoulder look while walking away, twisting back from a driver's
seat, head thrown back against a reclining body. Every mannequin frame is square or clean profile.
OBSERVED, n=14.

### A7 · Permit ungraceful posture
`knees pulled up to chest, arms wrapped around knees`, `reclines in a bright green portable folding camp
chair`, `mid stride and slightly out of step`. Bodies at rest are lumpy and asymmetric. OBSERVED.

### A8 · Put incidental life in the background
`blurred crowds walking at the far kerb` · `other people walking in background` · `Pedestrians visible and
blurred` · and, explicitly, `A person with a fishing rod visible in the far background, **adding life
depth**`. Three of seven LUXRY prompts do it and the author names the reason. This is P2 for figures: a
photograph records a world that was busy without the subject. CORPUS.

### A9 · Negate the AI tells by name
Set2 carries a negative prompt in 7 of 7, and across them: plastic-skin family **7/7**, AI-glow family
**7/7**, extra fingers or limbs **7/7**, watermark **7/7**, illustration **7/7**, a studio backdrop or flash
**5/7**. Short, and every item is something that could plausibly appear — P8. CORPUS.

### A10 · Muted head to toe, one accent
The soul deck again: *"Muted head to toe with one accent. Two colours and it stops looking Tokyo."* Each
frame's strap names its palette — `BLUE + KHAKI`, `ALL BLACK`, `OXBLOOD + CREAM`, `RED ACCENT`. Same lever as
the product decks' "the product is the only saturated thing", pointed at wardrobe. CORPUS.

## The garment sub-lever the soul deck is actually about

Volume is specified as a verb, not an adjective. `extremely wide` appears 11 times in 8 prompts, and it is
always followed by what the hem does: **pooling** (3), **dragging** (2), **stacking** (1), plus *puddling*.
The deck's own rule: *"Say extremely wide, then say what the hem does. Pool, stack or drag."*

Generalise it: **a garment adjective plus the physical consequence of that adjective.** Oversized plus how
the shoulder seam falls. Cropped plus what it exposes when she lifts an arm.

## Spinoff template

Everything in brackets is yours. This is the soul shape, which is the shortest of the three and the only one
whose images I could not check, so treat the structure as CORPUS and the output quality as untested here.

```
[Street style / candid lifestyle] phone snapshot of [who], [where and what they are physically doing],
[the hand action, specific], [the expression, and it is not a smile]. They wear [garment + the physical
consequence of its cut], [second garment], [one accent item]. Behind them: [three real objects, named],
[the incidental life — people, traffic, movement, blurred]. [Flat / available / ambient light source],
[colour cast], slight phone camera distortion, mild grain. [Crop], [full body or waist up], candid
[place] mood.
```

Rules that come with it:
- Muted throughout, exactly one accent colour.
- No lighting instrument may be named. If you name a softbox you are making a different picture.
- One gesture repeated across the set, varied in every frame.
- No smile unless something is causing it.
- At least one thing in the background that has nothing to do with the subject.

## Where this sits against the rest of the skill

`figure-prompts.md` is the descriptive register and `fashionnn-corpus.md` is the labelled-block corpus. This
file is the **behavioural** layer — it is about what the body is doing, not how the prompt is formatted, so
it applies to either format. Load it with `figure-prompts.md` for any brief where a person has to look real
rather than look good.

For a person **holding a product**, aliveness is necessary but not sufficient: scale is a separate unsolved
problem and the route to it is a plate, not an adjective. See `plate-pipeline.md` and `size-lock.md`.

## Break tests

- A4: same prompt with `smiling warmly` versus a named non-smile.
- A3: same scene with `flat overcast daylight` versus a softbox rig.
- A2: with and without the distortion-and-grain clause.
- A6: square to camera versus torso turned away from the face direction.
- The SKIN correlation: the same frame with `natural pores visible, minimal retouching` versus
  `glass skin finish, pore-minimised`.
