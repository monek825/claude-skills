# Deck composition — what the seven slots actually are

Derived by reading **13 complete decks / 87 frames** in the reference library — the folders that carry a
full carousel (contact sheet + promo card + poster), so each is one finished campaign: Rolex, Dior, Aesop,
L'Occitane, Matchaeologist, North Face, Rhode, Lacoste, Anua, Cosmix, Kissan, SuperYou, and a fragrance deck.
Counts below are my classification of those 87 frames, not his stated intent.

## The ratio, measured

**Every deck carries one or two conceit frames and five or six grounded ones.** Not three, not four. This is
the mixed-list rule from `invention.md` showing up in his actual practice: the strange frame is memorable
because the rest are not strange. A deck of seven conceits is a pure list and reads as noise.

Build order: **lock the conceit first** — it is the hardest and it sets the register — then fill the grounded
slots around it. Grounded does not mean lazy; it means the idea is carried by craft rather than by a twist.

## The slots

### Tier 1 — the conceit (1–2 per deck, mandatory)

**1. Register-distance conceit.** The `invention.md` engine's output. Product truth said by an object from the
wrong world. *Rolex among goldfish · Rolex belted to a moka pot · jam jar suspended in a net bag of fruit ·
gloss floating among clear gel domes.*

### Tier 2 — the product's own substance (1–2 per deck)

**2. Formula-as-set.** The product stands in a landscape of *itself* — its own cream, powder, jam, foam,
sculpted into terrain. Answers "what's inside" without a cutaway. *Rhode blush ploughing a trench through a
bed of loose pigment · L'Occitane on a thick S-curve of its own cream · fragrance in a mound of whipped cream.*

**3. Ingredient-as-terrain.** *(Not previously in this skill.)* The product's key note or ingredient, made
physical and then **repeated across the environment** until it becomes the ground. *Lacoste Original standing
on a padel court strewn with a dozen whole oranges — the citrus note as literal terrain — with one orange
blown huge and out of focus in the foreground as a framing device.* Distinct from a prop tableau: the
ingredient is not *beside* the product, it *is* the floor.

**4. Output-as-hero.** *(Not previously in this skill.)* The hero is not the product but **what the product
produces**, wearing the brand mark. *Kissan: a slice of white bread thickly spread with jam, the wordmark
embossed into the jam's surface in relief; the jar is demoted to a supporting prop at the back.* This is
replacement in the Phillips-McQuarrie sense and it is the strongest branding device in the library — the mark
appears on the *result*, not the pack.

### Tier 3 — the grounded backbone (4–5 per deck)

**5. In-hand body contact.** Product against skin, at the temple, at the collarbone, at the mouth, cap
pressed into a cheek so it dimples. Face usually cropped. **Two per deck is his norm** and it is the single
most common frame in the whole library.

**6. Environment that carries the positioning.** A real place whose genre states the brand's world. *Aesop in
a limestone shower niche with hard sun cutting a diagonal · Aesop on a worn washstand before a steamed window
· matcha at a laminate diner table · matcha beside a turntable.* This is the "place that matches the object's
vibe" slot — one or two per deck.

**7. Buyer-as-objects tableau.** The customer drawn as their possessions, no person in frame. **Present in
almost every deck, and the most underrated slot.** *L'Occitane spilling from a lizard-embossed handbag with a
silver flip phone, a compact camera, folded dollars · Rolex on a shelf between a blue plastic comb and a
brass fork · Rhode on a takeaway coffee cup with wired earbuds looped around it.* Dates, places and prices
the buyer without a face.

**8. Macro formula texture.** Extreme close crop where the surface *is* the subject — product melting over
its own shoulder, powder grain, iridescent bubbles backlit. One per deck.

**9. Pour / liquid architecture.** Gravity doing the work: a stream landing on the product, tins caught
mid-pour, gloss strands falling the full height of frame. ~One per deck.

**10. Flatlay / kit.** Overhead into an open bag, tray or pouch. Says "what you carry". ~One per deck.

**11. Pair-set.** Two or three units in one frame — one standing, one lying, one open. Shows two faces of the
pack and fills a composition without importing a foreign prop. ~One per deck.

**12. Worn.** Affordance-driven and dominant when the product is worn: the North Face deck is **four of seven**
worn frames. For non-worn products this slot collapses into 5.

### Tier 4 — devices, not slots (use at most one per deck)

These are treatments you apply *to* a slot, not frames in their own right:

- **Selective colour.** Environment desaturated to grey, subject and product alone in full saturation.
  *(SuperYou supermarket.)* A post device used as the whole concept.
- **Monochrome world.** An entire built environment in one hue, product included. *(SuperYou pink locker
  room — lockers, bench, jacket, headphones, dumbbells, all pink.)*
- **Confection / maximalism.** Heavily confected staging: a hand pushing up through a hole cut in the
  tabletop, fog spilling out, product and debris suspended, sparkle overlay, splash frozen mid-air.
  *(SuperYou.)* High risk — reads as AI slop unless every element is motivated.
- **Foreground-blur framing.** An out-of-focus object of the same kind occupying a whole corner, framing the
  sharp product behind it. *(Lacoste's giant orange.)* Cheap depth, very effective.
- **Retro-object contrast.** A period object cropped bold beside the product — but the specific objects in
  the corpus (rotary phone, alarm clock, turntable, typewriter) are on the do-not-restage list in
  `signature.md`. Choose from the buyer's world instead.

## A build sheet for seven frames

| # | Slot | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Register-distance conceit | The frame people remember and repost |
| 02 | Formula-as-set *or* ingredient-as-terrain | Proves what is inside |
| 03 | In-hand body contact | Scale, skin, use |
| 04 | Environment carrying the positioning | Where this product lives |
| 05 | Buyer-as-objects tableau | Who buys it, without a face |
| 06 | Macro formula texture *or* pour | Craft and desire |
| 07 | Flatlay / kit *or* pair-set | Range and portability |

Swap 03 for **worn** when the product is worn. Add a second conceit only by replacing 06 or 07 — never by
displacing the grounded slots, which are what make the conceit legible.

## Grounded does not mean literal-with-nothing

The user's instinct — "literal product photography, maybe floating on a premium background, or placed in an
environment matching the object's vibe" — maps onto slots 4, 6 and 11, and those are correct. But note what
the measured data says: **clean studio hero and mirror-plinth are among the rarest frames in his decks.**
The grounded backbone is overwhelmingly *in-hand* and *environment*, not floating packshots. A floating
product on a gradient is the easiest frame to specify and the least distinctive to look at; spend it once, or
not at all.
