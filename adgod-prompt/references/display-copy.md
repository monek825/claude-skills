# Display copy — short, bold, built for tall condensed type

For poster-register ads where the headline is set huge in a condensed display face and the image carries no
text of its own. This is not landing-page copy and not conversion copy; those are different disciplines with
different rules (see the note at the bottom).

## The form

**Three to five words. Two stacked lines. All caps. One terminal mark.**

```
KABEL GPS
LAMA MATI?
```

- **Word count 3–5.** Six is already too many; the second line stops holding its width.
- **Two lines, similar width.** Condensed faces read as a block. Lines of wildly different length break the
  block and the type stops being the design.
- **Caps throughout.** Tall condensed faces are drawn for caps; lowercase descenders wreck the stacking.
- **One mark, at the end.** A full stop or a question mark. Never a comma inside the headline — a comma
  forces a pause the eye does not have time for at this size.
- **Split at the grammatical seam**, so each line survives being read alone.
- **No numerals unless the number is the drama.** `JAM 2 PAGI` earns its digit. A statistic does not.

## What goes in the big type, and what does not

| Layer | Size | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | huge condensed caps | The buyer's situation, 3–5 words |
| Resolution | small | What they get, one line |
| Offer | small | The entry point |
| Endline | small, footer | Brand line, verbatim, never altered |

Only the headline is display type. Putting the proposition in the big type at equal weight kills both — the
eye cannot rank two shouted things.

## Writing the headline

1. **Start from the situation, not the feature.** The headline is the moment the buyer is already in.
2. **Use their words.** Whatever they say to a colleague at 21:00 is the headline; whatever appears in the
   product menu is not.
3. **Two beats beat one.** The strongest form in this register is a pair — a setup line and a turn:
   `MESIN HIDUP. / RODA DIAM.` The two lines do the work a whole sentence would otherwise need.
4. **Cut every word that survives removal.** Read it without each word in turn. If the meaning holds, the
   word was scaffolding.
5. **Say it aloud.** Display copy is read at a glance but heard in the head. If it does not scan, it is long.

## When the poster is one half of a post

On social, the poster is not the whole asset — the caption is. That legitimises a form the poster alone
could not carry: an **open loop**. The display type states the situation, a short brand line refuses to
resolve it (`<Brand> solusinya.`), and the caption answers *how, and what proof*, ending in a contact.

If you use this, keep three things straight:
- The open loop and the brand endline are **different slots**. Loop in the middle, endline in the footer
  beside the logo. One creates the question; the other builds recall. They do not compete unless you make
  them share a position.
- The caption must actually **resolve** the loop with mechanism, not adjectives. A loop opened and left open
  is a bait.
- Any **qualifier** belongs in the caption beside the CTA, not on the poster — it has to sit where the reader
  decides whether to make contact.

## Do not build a hook on a condition the buyer may not share

The fastest way to lose a reader is a headline describing a situation that is not his. Check every hook
against the segment's real operating conditions before writing it: a fleet running around the clock hears
"at 2am" and concludes the ad is for someone else.

Prefer **audience-defined** framing — let the reader supply the threshold: `at the wrong time` /
`off schedule` / `without an order`, never a specific hour, season, or shift. Specificity is a virtue in
proof and a liability in premises.

## Test before shipping

- [ ] 3–5 words
- [ ] Splits into two lines of roughly equal width
- [ ] Each line still means something read alone
- [ ] No comma inside the headline
- [ ] Reads as something the buyer would actually say
- [ ] The proposition, offer and endline are in small type, not competing
- [ ] Nothing in it is a claim that needs evidence you do not have

## Why not a general copywriting skill

The published copywriting skills on GitHub (`coreyhaines31/marketingskills`, `boraoztunc/skills`,
`syntax-syndicate/marketing-skills`, and the copywriting entry in `borghei/Claude-Skills`) are landing-page
and long-form conversion guides — hero sections, social proof blocks, objection handling, page structure
frameworks. Checked 2026-08-30: they explicitly do not cover short display copy, posters, or out-of-home.
Installing one would import the wrong instincts, chiefly length. This page exists because that gap is real
and narrow.
