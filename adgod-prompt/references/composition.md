# Composition — where the product actually goes

Measured 2026-09-18 by classifying all **194 catalogued adgod outputs** in
`~/Documents/adgod-prompts/refs/adgod-ig/` and reading every one of them on contact sheets. This is the
answer to "why do ours still look generic when the lighting and grading are right."

## The number

Of his 194 outputs, **53 (27%) are poster layouts** and **141–143 are photographs**. Of those photographs:

| | |
|---|---|
| Product has at least one named prop | **95%** |
| A human present | **43%** |
| **Product alone — no prop, no human** | **0 of 143** |
| Median props per photograph | **3** |

**He has never once photographed the product alone in a void.** Not once in 143 frames.

That is exactly what our rejected frames were. v2 frames 01, 02, 03 and 05 — the four the user threw out —
were the bottle, its own liquid, and nothing else. The three he kept (04 hair, 06 oil-on-glass, 07 woman at a
window) were the three where something else was in contact with it.

A liquid effect is not a prop. Oil pouring onto the product is still the product alone.

## The rule

**The product must have a physical relationship with something that is not itself.** It rests on, leans
against, is buried in, draped by, clamped in, threaded through, balanced on, held by, or sunk into something.

"Standing upright and dead level on a plinth, centred, with an effect happening around it" is the single most
available composition and he does not use it.

## Orientation

Upright is the exception, not the default. Across the sheets he lies the product flat, tilts it, hangs it,
balances it at an angle, sinks it, and shoots it top-down at least as often as he stands it up. When it does
stand, something is touching it.

## The second output mode we never make

**27% of his feed is not a photograph at all.** It is a designed poster: cream paper ground, an enormous
serif wordmark bleeding off both edges, corner crop marks, small caption text set in the margins
(`GENERATED THROUGH PROMPTS`, `NO CAMERA. NO REAL SHOOT. ONLY PROMPTS.`), and the product composited in the
middle at a clean three-quarter angle.

**That is where "product alone and upright" is allowed** — because the layout is doing the composition. We
have been producing the centre of that poster with the poster removed, which is the worst of both: no
relationship in the frame, and no design around it.

If a frame is going to be a clean hero, it needs a layout. If it has no layout, it needs a relationship.

## Checklist before writing any product frame

- [ ] Name at least two physical objects in the frame besides the product. Median is three.
- [ ] Say what is **touching** the product, and where.
- [ ] Is it upright? If yes, justify it — otherwise lie it down, tilt it, sink it or hand it to someone.
- [ ] Is a human in it? Should be about 4 frames in 10 across a deck.
- [ ] If the product is genuinely alone, is this a **poster**? If not, add a relationship.
- [ ] Its own liquid does not count as a prop.
