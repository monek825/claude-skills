# Scene archetypes

> **This page names kinds of frame, not frames.** Every scene it lists is one adgod has published. Use it
> to identify what a deck is still missing, then build that frame from the product via `ideation.md` and
> `adaptation.md`. Lifting a scene from here reproduces his authorship — see `signature.md`.

The 62 corpus prompts collapse into 18 reusable scenes. Each names the source prompts to copy sentence
patterns from (`Deck — Prompt NN` in `corpus.md`), the camera package, and what it is good for.

A campaign set should mix **at least one from each of the four families** below so the deck has variety:
hero packshot · built still-life · human lifestyle · concept/effect.

**Weight the mix by what the sources actually are, not by the order of this page.** Counted across the 420
reference photographs in `library.md`: in-hand 109 · environment 102 · prop-tableau 57 — against
hero-packshot 14 and mirror-plinth 7. Family 1 opens this document because it is the easiest to specify, not
because it is the common case. It earns roughly one frame in a seven-frame deck. Held-in-hand and
in-the-world frames are the backbone.

## Family 1 — Hero packshot (product alone, studio)

### A1. Clean floating hero
Product upright/centred on seamless gradient, nothing else in frame. Maximum negative space.
Source: Dior 01 · Nothing 04 · Nothing 05
Camera: Phase One + 120mm LS Macro, ISO 50, f/11, focus stacked
Best for: launch announcement, first slide of a deck, e-commerce hero.

### A2. Mirror-plinth hero
Product on a highly polished reflective acrylic or glossy surface producing a soft mirror reflection beneath.
Source: Chamberlain 05 · Liquid Death 02 · Rhode 02
Camera: Phase One + macro, f/11
Best for: premium CPG, cosmetics line-ups, anything with a strong silhouette.

### A3. Pedestal / balance hero
Product balanced or resting on a rugged natural object (volcanic stone, rock stack, log) against a clean studio gradient — luxury-vs-raw tension.
Source: Nothing 07 · North Face 01 · Liquid Death 06
Camera: Phase One + 120mm Macro, f/11 (studio) or full-frame 85mm f/1.8 (nature)
Best for: durability, outdoor, "engineered" positioning.

### A4. Macro texture / surface hero
Extreme close-up, product fills the frame diagonally corner-to-corner, surface treatment is the subject (droplets, cream, powder, condensation).
Source: Nothing 04 · Rhode 07 · Rhode 05
Camera: Phase One or Hasselblad + macro, f/11
Best for: formula, finish, waterproofing, material claims.

## Family 2 — Built still-life (product + curated props)

### B1. Editorial prop tableau
Hero product centre, 4–6 named props arranged with explicit spatial anchors, warm seamless backdrop.
Source: Chamberlain 01 · Chamberlain 06 · Matcha 07 · Rolex 04 · Rolex 07
Camera: Phase One + 80mm or 120mm, f/8–f/11
Best for: ritual, lifestyle context, gift guides.

### B2. Ingredient / build story
Product on one side, an ingredient structure (caramel cube tower, chocolate stack, fruit cluster, pour) on the other. Product is hero, ingredient is supporting narrative.
Source: Chamberlain 05 · Chamberlain 03 · Rhode 03
Camera: 100mm macro, f/11
Best for: flavour, formulation, "what's inside".

### B3. Flat-lay / tray
Top-down or 35–45° overhead. Product on a tray, in a pouch, or laid on a surface with satellite objects.
Source: Dior 04 · Rhode 03 · Matcha 01 · Matcha 03
Camera: Hasselblad X2D + 100mm macro, f/8
Best for: "what's in the bag", collections, kits.

### B4. Surreal object stack
Product integrated into an impossible-but-elegant arrangement. **Derive the arrangement from the product's own material and benefit — never reuse the corpus examples, which are adgod's signature frames and are on the do-not-restage list in `signature.md`.**
Source: Dior 06 · Rolex 05 · Rolex 06 · Rhode 04
Camera: Hasselblad 90mm f/11 or Phase One 120mm f/11
Best for: art-direction-forward hero, editorial covers, standing out in a feed.

### B5. Retro-object contrast
A large vintage object cropped bold in the foreground, product beside it. **Choose the object from the buyer's world, not from the corpus's — the rotary phone, alarm clock, turntable and typewriter are his and are on the do-not-restage list in `signature.md`.**
Source: Dior 03 · Chamberlain 01 · Matcha 06
Camera: Phase One 80mm, f/8
Best for: heritage, nostalgia, "timeless" claims.

### B6. Set-dressed environment
A miniature built environment on a studio floor (camp set, café table, desk) with the product placed in it.
Source: North Face 07 · Liquid Death 07 · Matcha 04
Camera: Phase One 80mm, f/8, overhead spotlight pool
Best for: use-case storytelling without leaving the studio.

## Family 3 — Human lifestyle

### C1. Beauty portrait, product at face
Tight portrait, model holds product near cheek/chin/temple, eyes and product both tack sharp.
Source: Dior 02 · Dior 07 · Rhode 01 · Rhode 06
Camera: Hasselblad X2D + XCD 90V or 90mm, f/8
Best for: cosmetics, skincare, anything applied to the body.

### C2. Consumption / in-use portrait
Model actively drinking, wearing, or operating the product; hand and product interaction is the story.
Source: Chamberlain 04 · Chamberlain 07 · Liquid Death 03 · Oakley 04
Camera: 85mm portrait, f/5.6–f/8
Best for: refreshment, daily ritual, ease of use.

### C3. Worn-product hero (back/profile)
Model from behind or in profile with the product worn (backpack, eyewear, earbud). Low angle, heroic.
Source: North Face 02 · North Face 03 · North Face 05 · Nothing 02 · Oakley 02
Camera: Phase One 120mm or medium telephoto, f/8
Best for: apparel, carry, wearables.

### C4. Athletic / performance
Athlete mid-effort outdoors, low wide angle, hard directional sun, saturated sky.
Source: Oakley 03 · Oakley 05 · Oakley 06
Camera: Phase One + wide-angle, f/8, ISO 100
Best for: sport, endurance, performance claims.

### C5. Environmental lifestyle location
Model on location (cliff, snowfield, coast, road) with product naturally in the scene, huge negative space.
Source: North Face 04 · North Face 02 · Oakley 05
Camera: medium format, f/8, natural light
Best for: aspiration, travel, brand-film stills.

### C6. Direct-flash candid
On-camera flash, crouched or hand-forward pose, dark cinematic sky, film grain. Reads as street/social, not studio.
Source: Liquid Death 05
Camera: full-frame, 24–35mm, shallow-to-moderate DoF
Best for: youth brands, energy, "anti-luxury" luxury.

### C7. Studio white full-body carry
Model cropped torso-to-feet on seamless white, product held at full arm length in front of legs.
Source: North Face 06 · Nothing 03
Camera: medium format, clean high-key
Best for: bags, carry goods, footwear, catalogue-plus.

## Family 4 — Concept / effect

### D1. Liquid architecture
Controlled glossy liquid ribbons or streams wrapping the product, vertical strands framing it. Graphic, not messy.
Source: Dior 05 · Rhode 07 · Chamberlain 05
Camera: Hasselblad 120mm macro, f/11, focus stacking
Best for: hydration, texture, formula.

### D2. Frozen splash / impact
Crown-shaped water explosion suspended behind the product at the moment of maximum impact.
Source: Oakley 01
Camera: Phase One 120mm macro, f/11, ISO 100, strobe + simulated sunlight
Best for: refreshment, cooling, waterproofing.

### D3. Submerged / optical distortion
Product inside a glass of carbonated liquid, magnified and refracted through the glass wall.
Source: Liquid Death 01
Camera: Phase One + macro, focus-stacked, black backdrop
Best for: purity, carbonation, water claims.

### D4. Pour / stream
Product tilted 45° from the frame edge pouring into a vessel, high-speed liquid detail.
Source: Liquid Death 04 · Chamberlain 05
Camera: Phase One macro, high-speed liquid capture
Best for: serve suggestion, decanting rituals.

### D5. Deformation / stress
Product physically crushed, compressed, or embedded, with branding surviving across the deformation.
Source: Liquid Death 02 · Rhode 05
Camera: Phase One macro, f/11
Best for: attitude brands, recyclability, durability.

### D6. Levitation / repetition
Product multiplied or suspended with no visible support, perfect symmetry.
Source: Nothing 05 · Matcha 05
Camera: Phase One 120mm macro, f/11
Best for: tech, systems, "the set" rather than one unit.

## Family 5 — Industrial / CMF

A distinct register rather than a set of scenes: soft grey seamless, near-shadowless overcast key, one
saturated accent, materials named precisely. Full spec and five prompts in `industrial-cmf.md`.

### E1. Exploded float
Assemblies suspended at different depths along a diagonal, tilt-shift focal plane crossing the frame.
Best for: architecture, modularity, "what it's made of".

### E2. Knurl / detail macro
Extreme close on one control, near knurls critically sharp, everything else dissolving within a centimetre.
Best for: build quality, tactility, precision claims.

### E3. Backlit accent
A concealed source throws saturated colour through and around the object so the accent reads as light, not
paint. Best for: a single hero colour, translucency, "it lights up".

### E4. Held device, dark field
Hand from the bottom edge, product proud of the grip, background darker than the subject so it separates
without a rim light. Best for: scale, ergonomics, everyday use.

### E5. CMF hero
Classic three-quarter, tipped back, floating over a soft elliptical contact shadow. Best for: the
establishing shot of a hardware launch.

**Do not mix this family into a campaign running another register.** It is a complete visual system, not a
scene you can borrow — using one of these beside a warm-vignette or high-key set reads as two campaigns.

## Picking a set for one campaign

A seven-image deck that mirrors how the source decks are built:

1. A1 or A2 — clean hero (the establishing shot)
2. B1 or B3 — prop tableau or flat-lay (context)
3. C1, C2 or C3 — human interaction (desire)
4. D1–D6 — one concept/effect image (the scroll-stopper)
5. B2 or B5 — ingredient or heritage story (proof)
6. C4, C5 or C6 — environment or energy (world)
7. A3 or A4 — texture/pedestal close (craft)

Keep one palette family and one lighting logic across all seven or the deck reads as stock.
