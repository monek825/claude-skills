# Hooks

## The job
In the first 1–3 seconds the viewer must answer two questions: **what is this about** and **what do I get if I keep watching**. Everything else is decoration.

## 4 ingredients (all required)
1. **Subject**: a clear, named topic (a brand name, "your bio link", "your snack packet").
2. **Stakes**: why the viewer should care (what they lose or gain).
3. **Speed**: as few words as possible. Under 15 spoken words; the first sentence under about 4 seconds out loud.
4. **Clarity**: only one possible meaning. No puns that need decoding.

## Three hooks at once (they must agree)
- **Spoken hook**: the first sentence of the voiceover.
- **Text hook** (most important, because most viewers start on mute):
  - on screen for about 3 s, at most two lines
  - placed about a third of the way down the frame, away from the Reels UI
  - a punchy fragment, not a sentence; no punctuation except quotes or parentheses
  - specific numbers ("22 hari" beats "beberapa minggu")
- **Visual hook**: the first frame is already moving: mid-action, an object entering, text animating, a camera push. Never a logo, a slow establishing shot or a greeting.

Misalignment between these three is the most common hook failure.

## Formulas (pick one, write 3–5 variations, test)
| Formula | Shape | Example (Breq-style) |
|---|---|---|
| Bad news | "Ada kabar buruk." + the specific problem | "Ada kabar buruk. Link bio kamu lempar pembeli ke chat kosong." |
| The test | Give the viewer something to do right now | "Buka link bio kamu di hape. Hitung sampai produknya kelihatan." |
| Callout | Name the exact viewer | "Yang jualan keripik di Shopee, ini buat kamu." |
| Stop doing this | Name the mistake | "Berhenti taruh nomor halal di dalam gambar." |
| Open loop | A question only the video answers | "Kenapa pembeli nanya 'ini asli nggak, Kak?' terus?" |
| Payoff first | Show the result, then rewind | First frame: WhatsApp opens with the order already typed. "Ini pesanan yang nulis sendiri." |
| Contrarian | A claim against common belief | "Website kamu nggak perlu cerita sejarah perusahaan." |
| Stakes | What leaving costs | "Selama kamu baca ini, pembelinya udah pindah." |
| Mid-action | Start in the middle of the event | A hand already scanning a crumpled packet: "Bungkus ini masih bisa jualan." |
| Confession (UMKM favourite) | A personal admission | "Pelanggan aku pernah komplain gara-gara hal ini." |

## The lock-in (the 2–4 sentences after the hook)
Confirm the hook's promise immediately, so the viewer doesn't feel tricked. Then earn trust with a concrete detail, a visible screen recording or a real example.

A bold hook with no proof in the next 5 seconds loses the viewer at the sag point, about 5–8 s in.

## Hook failure modes
- **Delay**: the topic arrives after second 3 ("Halo semua, hari ini…"). Cut everything before the first useful word.
- **Confusion**: two possible meanings, or a clever pun. Say it plainly.
- **Irrelevance**: hooks a crowd that isn't the buyer. A meme hook for snack owners pulls teenagers.
- **Disinterest**: no stakes. Add what the viewer loses or gains.
- **Bait**: the video doesn't deliver what the hook promised. People drop off and stop trusting the account.
- **Fake suspense** ("wait for it", "you won't believe"): audiences have learned to scroll past it.

## Testing hooks
Keep the body identical. Record or edit only the first 3 seconds in 2–3 versions and post them as separate videos over time. The number that judges a hook is retention at about the 3-second mark in Insights, not likes.
