# Structures

Timings are for a 30–60 s reel. Scale them proportionally. Whatever the template, the middle must unfold: each beat answers one question and opens the next. Never explain in one long block.

## 1. Hook, Lock, Retain, Reward (default)
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Subject + stakes, three hooks aligned |
| Lock | 3–8 s | Confirm the promise, show proof, minimum context only |
| Retain | 8 s to about 80% | Pay off the hook in small steps; each step opens a small loop ("masalah kedua lebih parah"); visual change every 2–5 s |
| Reward | last 15–25% | The punchline, result, answer or transformation |
| CTA / loop | last 2–4 s | One clear next step, or an ending that cuts back into the first frame |

## 2. Problem, Agitate, Fix (value videos)
1. **Problem** (the hook): one concrete, recognisable situation ("pembeli kirim 'kak ini masih ada?' dan kamu harus jawab satu-satu").
2. **Agitate**: show the cost in a scene, not a statistic (the chat flood, the buyer leaving).
3. **Fix**: the mechanism, shown on screen (the pre-filled order message).
4. **Proof or try-it**: something the viewer can do or see themselves.
5. **CTA** (optional): one line.

## 3. The "bad news" spine (from the SteelSeries Sensei/Rival Pro ad, about 90 s, 2M+ organic likes)
Their beats, reusable for any product:
1. Bad news ("I got bad news.")
2. Reveal a hidden problem the viewer can verify ("See this raw cursor input? It reveals your tracking can skip.")
3. The consequence, with an ego sting ("That gap should've been a headshot… some random kid thinks he's better than you.")
4. "This is why we made X", then the plain mechanism: cause, fix, benefit.
5. The objection as a joke ("With great power comes great power consumption"), then the fix.
6. A repetition gag ("keep beating kids on and on. And on.")
7. An inclusive callout with one weird group ("big, small, and claw grip weirdos").
8. Remove the excuse ("when you whiff, it's not the mouse. It's you.")
9. The honest twist ("If you suck you'll still suck. But if you're good, you could be great.")
10. A two-word tag (FOR GLORY).

Why it works:
- Honesty and self-deprecation lower the viewer's guard.
- The product is the payoff, not the pitch.
- It has lines people send to friends (claw grip weirdos, "it's you, Jon").

## 4. Value series (one value per video, 15–25 s, repeatable weekly)
The hook (the situation), then the mechanism on screen, then one line on why it matters, then an optional CTA. Keep the same intro style, music and text treatment every week, so viewers recognise the series.

## 5. Deadpan comedy
- **The mismatch is the joke:** plain, flat wording in an absurd situation. The delivery alone isn't funny.
- **Never wink:** no "lol" text, no comic sound effects that announce the joke, no music that tells the viewer to laugh.
- **Repeat and escalate:** the same phrase returns, each time with more at stake.
- **The product stays quiet:** it is the payoff or a quiet presence, never the pitch.
- **Make the device repeatable:** a character like "Si Jempol" or a running line, so one funny video becomes a series.
- **No explaining:** if the joke needs explaining, cut it.

## 6. Offer reel (bottom of funnel, for people who already know the brand)
Keep it under 30 s:
1. The hook (the result).
2. What they get, shown as 3–4 quick concrete moments, not a feature list.
3. The risk removed (a free demo, a revision).
4. One CTA with an exact action ("DM 'DEMO'").

## Ratio
Talk about the viewer's problem roughly twice as much as about yourself. The brand appears as the cause of the fix, in one or two lines.

## Endings
- **Cut right after the payoff.** No "thanks for watching".
- **Loop:** end on a frame or line that flows into the opening (black to black, or a last line that sets up the first), so replays happen naturally and add watch time.
- **One-action CTA,** in words the viewer can type ("DM DEMO", "komentar angkanya").
