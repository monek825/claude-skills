# Research notes (11 Oct 2026)

Each claim is tagged with how strong it is:
- **[platform]**: said by Instagram or TikTok themselves
- **[consensus]**: repeated by many independent creator and marketing sources
- **[anecdote]**: a single source or a vendor claim, so treat it as a hint only

## Algorithm (what the script is graded on)
- [platform] Instagram's Mosseri (Jan 2025): the top Reels signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Likes weigh slightly more with followers; sends weigh slightly more with non-followers.
- [platform] Watch time counts both the percentage watched and the seconds watched (Feb 2025), so longer videos aren't automatically penalised.
- [platform] Original content is favoured over reposts.
- [anecdote] "Sends are worth 3–5× likes", "20-minute Reels", "10 reposts and you're excluded": no primary source found. Don't rely on them.

## Hooks and retention
- [consensus] Most drop-off happens in the first seconds. The hook must state the topic and the reason to stay within about 3 s.
- [consensus] Spoken, text and visual hooks must align. The text hook carries the most weight because viewers start on mute.
- [consensus] Change something visual every 2–5 s. The sag zone is about 5–8 s in, so the lock-in (confirming the promise and adding proof) must land there.
- [consensus] Open small loops through the middle, but don't nest too many.
- [consensus] End on the payoff and cut. Endings that flow back into the opening encourage replays.
- [consensus] Test hooks by changing only the first 3 s, and judge them by 3-second retention, not likes.
- [anecdote] Specific percentages ("67% swipe in 3 s", "pattern interrupts cut drop-off 20–40%") come from vendor blogs and conflict with each other.

## Kallaway-style hook rules (from a transcript of his talk)
- The 4 ingredients: subject, stakes, speed and clarity.
- The text hook: about a third of the way down the frame, two lines maximum, a plain font, specific numbers, no transition words, about 3 s on screen.
- The lock-in zone: confirm the promise right after the hook, and earn trust through presentation, packaging, relatability and proof.

## Comedy and brand
- [consensus] Deadpan works through the gap between the situation and the reaction. The brand shouldn't wink or explain the joke.
- [consensus] Repetition with escalation and a repeatable device turn one hit into a series.
- [consensus] Humour that doesn't fit the brand confuses people. The joke must come from the product's real situation.

## Indonesia / UMKM
- [consensus, Indonesian sources] Hooks that work for UMKM audiences:
  - personal confessions ("pelanggan aku pernah komplain…")
  - specific small numbers ("modal 20 ribu")
  - before-and-after
  - edutainment
- [anecdote, thesis] Creative visual themes outperformed purely informational videos for a small UMKM account. Storytelling, a hook and a CTA together improved engagement.

## Existing skills reviewed
- **`viral-reel-generator`** (Beerspitnight/cc-skills, MIT).
  - Useful parts: an anti-slop list (no three-word loops, rhetorical lists, meta commentary or hype adjectives), a "mechanism" rule, and discovery questions for vague briefs.
  - Not adopted: its "Connector" rule ("See, / Meaning, / Therefore"), because forced connectors read as a formula in Indonesian.
- **marketing-skills `copywriting` + `video`, and the local `stop-slop` skill:** the base rules this skill builds on.
