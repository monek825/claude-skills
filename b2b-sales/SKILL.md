# B2B sales

Source: a Reddit r/b2b_sales post ("10 years of b2b sales", shared as a carousel, Oct 2026). It is one practitioner's experience, not research. Treat its numbers ("5–8 touches", "11 months") as anecdotes.

## The 11 principles, and what each means for Breq

| # | Principle | Apply it at Breq |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **People buy from whoever they trust most, not whoever has the best product.** Lead with questions and listening. | First messages show you looked at *their* business: one checked observation and one question. The mockup is proof that you listened, not a features pitch. |
| 2 | **The money is in the follow-up.** Deals over about US$5k often take 5–8 touches. Every touch must add something new. Never write "just checking in" or "mau follow up". | Each follow-up brings one new thing: a gap you found on their site, a mobile screenshot, an idea for their reseller page, or a short relevant fact. **The current house rule allows one follow-up after 4+ days. Don't add more touches without Bray's yes.** |
| 3 | **Cold outreach still works,** but only as small, targeted batches with good deliverability and a real reason to care. | Keep the Tue–Thu cap (20 a day). Quality per lead beats volume. Use email plus Instagram DM; never cold WhatsApp. |
| 4 | **Price is rarely the real reason you lost.** It's the polite exit. "If they're telling you it's about price, you already lost on value." | When a lead goes quiet after seeing the price, don't cut the price. Ask one honest question about what held them back, then fix the value story or the trust. |
| 5 | **The best salespeople aren't salesy.** No fake urgency, no pressure, no objection-handling games. | No "promo berakhir hari ini", no invented scarcity. Make yes easy: a clear next step, honest answers, the 50/50 payment. |
| 6 | **Nobody reads long proposals; they skim and jump to the price.** Keep it to 2–3 pages. | An offer reply has four parts: the problem we saw, what we'll build, the price, and the next step with a date. Fit it on one phone screen if you can. |
| 7 | **Talk to whoever can say yes.** Ask how decisions like this usually get made. | For food brands, it's usually the owner. For B2B/PT leads, ask "Biasanya keputusan seperti ini diputuskan oleh siapa saja?" and offer a 15-minute call with the decision maker. |
| 8 | **Your pipeline is lying to you.** No reply in 2 weeks means dead until proven otherwise. Ghosted after a proposal means lost. | Every Friday, review each lead in about 20 minutes: "Would I bet my own money this closes this month?" If not, move it to lost or nurture. Count *interested replies*, not sends. |
| 9 | **Walk away from bad deals early.** Qualify on four things: a real problem, the budget, a reason to act now, and the power to decide. | If any of the four is missing, say politely that it may not be the right fit, and move on. Watch for people who only want free consulting or a mockup to show their current vendor. |
| 10 | **Nobody cares about your company; they care about their problem.** Open with their problem, explained better than they could explain it themselves. | Keep the ratio around 2:1, their problem to Breq. Give the studio's story one line at most. |
| 11 | **Sales is a learnable skill, not a personality.** Introverts close through competence and listening. | Bray doesn't have to act like a salesman. Clear and helpful wins. |

## Checks before sending any sales message
- Does it open with *their* situation, not Breq?
- Does it carry one new, checked fact or idea, rather than "cuma mau follow up"?
- Does it contain zero invented numbers, zero fake urgency and zero pressure?
- Does it ask one clear question, or give one next step?
- Is it short enough to read on a phone in about 20 seconds?
- Is the person able to say yes? If not, does the message help reach whoever can?

## Follow-up ideas that add value (pick one per touch)
1. A mobile screenshot of their current site, with one specific fix.
2. The finding from their own value-audit row (no link preview, no WhatsApp button, halal number not shown).
3. A small extra idea for their mockup, such as a reseller page or a QR code for the packaging.
4. An answer to a question they asked earlier, that you've now checked.
5. A polite close: "Kalau belum waktunya, tidak apa-apa. Boleh saya hubungi lagi bulan depan?" This keeps trust and makes the pipeline honest.

## House rules still apply
Indonesian copy, "Kak" for food brands and "Bapak/Ibu" for B2B leads, no emojis, no price in the first message, no invented facts, and Bray's explicit yes before anything is sent or scheduled.
