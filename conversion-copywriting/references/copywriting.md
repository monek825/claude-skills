# Copywriting decisions

Read for messages, offers, ads, landing-page copy, and editing. This is a practical synthesis of advertising research, not a guaranteed conversion formula. Historical rules need adaptation to audience and channel.

## Brief before language

Establish the buyer's actual situation, desired outcome, current alternative (including doing nothing), offer/scope, strongest truthful reason to choose, available proof, main objection, audience knowledge, channel, and next step. Reuse research rather than inventing a persona.

Prefer accounts of actual purchases and behavior over hypothetical willingness. Ask what triggered the search, what alternatives were considered, what almost stopped the purchase, and what convinced the buyer. Keep customer phrases tied to their source/date. A vivid quote is not a representative survey. Publish identifiable quotations only with appropriate permission.

Write an offer plainly: who it helps, what they receive, why it is useful, what is included, price or pricing basis when relevant, material conditions, and the next step. A better offer can reduce uncertainty or effort without a discount. Do not hide decision-critical costs behind persuasive language.

## Match the argument to the reader

Schwartz's awareness framework is a planning lens, not a measurement of every visitor:

| Starting point | Useful approach |
|---|---|
| Does not recognize the problem | Show a recognizable situation or demonstration. |
| Recognizes the problem | Explain a plausible solution and its relevance. |
| Knows the solution category | Clarify the approach, fit, and reasons to choose. |
| Knows this product | Provide evidence, address doubts, and explain terms. |
| Ready to decide | Make the offer and next step easy to evaluate. |

Choose one main idea. When strategy is requested, compare distinct angles: desired outcome, credible mechanism, or important objection. Synonym swaps are not independent ideas. Adapt the argument and depth to the reader's task.

Direct-response copy supports a current action; brand advertising can build recognition and associations for later buying situations. State the campaign's primary job before judging it by clicks.

## Make the promise believable

Pair important claims with matching evidence: a demonstration for a workflow, measured records for time saved, documented context for a commercial case, or an accurately represented customer account. A testimonial is not universal proof. Explain the mechanism when it helps understanding; omit proprietary detail the reader does not need.

Use concrete nouns and verbs. Relevant specificity helps; unsupported precision does not. Connect useful features to outcomes, but retain technical details when they inform a comparison or substantiate the promise.

## Structure and edit

For a landing page, use the reader's questions to choose order: promise/situation → offer and fit → proof/demo → how it works → objections/terms → action is one possible sequence. Change it for existing buyers, unfamiliar categories, or research-led browsing. Avoid mandatory section counts and generic filler grids.

AIDA is a drafting checklist for attention, interest, desire and action, not proof that all visitors follow a linear funnel. Earn attention relevant to the offer; resolve the curiosity you create.

Edit in four passes:

1. Meaning: can an unfamiliar reader understand the offer?
2. Evidence: can factual promises be supported?
3. Flow: does information arrive when the reader needs it?
4. Language: remove abstractions, repetition and inflated praise without flattening voice.

Ask a fresh reader what the page offers, who it suits, why they believe it, and what happens next. Confusion signals a problem; successful comprehension does not establish increased sales.

Prefer a CTA that describes the actual step: “Request a project quote,” “View example projects,” or “Start a trial” only when that is the destination. Keep the message consistent from ad to page to confirmation. Do not invent response times, trial conditions, or an action endpoint.

## Original illustration

Weak: “Unlock your potential with premium digital experiences.”

Clearer: “A website that explains your service and makes enquiries easy.”

Add real audience fit, scope, example work, and quotation process. Do not turn this generic illustration into a guaranteed sales promise.

## Source trail and reading coverage

- [Hopkins, Scientific Advertising, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/): full accessible OCR read for the source research; specificity, selling and test discipline. Scan errors remain possible.
- [Caples, Tested Advertising Methods](https://books.google.com/books/about/Tested_Advertising_Methods.html?id=IPdCAAAAIAAJ): catalog/contents/snippets; complete edition not read.
- [Ogilvy on Advertising, Ogilvy-hosted edition](https://ogilvy.relayto.com/e/ogilvy-on-advertising-dq85bunnm5ady): selected passages across 20 chapters reviewed; product/customer research and execution, not complete cover-to-cover reading.
- [Schwartz, publisher's introduction](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/lp2/) and [chapter descriptions](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/ba-plus/): partial coverage, not full book reading.
- [Bly, publisher page/excerpt](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781250238009/thecopywritershandbook/): partial coverage.
- [Halbert chapter 6](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter6.htm) and [chapter 16](https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter16.htm): chapter text read; demand, relevant attention, explicit action.
- [Made to Stick, authorized sample](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780099505693/9780099505693-sample.pdf): introduction/framework read; core, concreteness and credibility, no guarantee.
- [Sharp on mental availability](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/mental-availability-is-not-awareness-brand-salience-is-not-awareness) and [IPA summary](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation): direct source explanations of memory and different time horizons.
- [Cialdini's official books](https://www.influenceatwork.com/books-and-publications/): persuasion should use genuine circumstances; no fabricated social proof or urgency.

References document intellectual inputs. The procedure above is original synthesis, not a quotation from any single author.
