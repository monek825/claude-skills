# Conversion copywriting

Optimize the reader's decision and the business outcome. A polished page, button click, or published case-study uplift does not establish profitable conversion on this site.

## Read only what the task needs

- Copy, messaging, offers, ads or editorial review: [references/copywriting.md](references/copywriting.md).
- Website structure, layout, typography, interaction or page audit: [references/website-design.md](references/website-design.md).
- Hero, offer, proof, pricing, process, FAQ, CTA or form decisions: [references/website-sections.md](references/website-sections.md).
- Industry/page fit: [references/website-types.md](references/website-types.md), then only the relevant subtype. Covers product showcases, F&B, logistics, software and other common sites.
- Experiment results, analytics, uplift claims or testing plans: [references/experiments.md](references/experiments.md).

For a full landing-page task, read copywriting and website design, then the relevant type and section guidance. For a focused edit, read only the needed material. Read experiments only when measuring or proposing tests. The references contain working guidance and primary links, not proof that a layout will win.

## Establish the actual decision

Use the user's brief and existing project context before asking questions. Read applicable `CLAUDE.md` and repository instructions. If present, reuse `.agents/product-marketing.md`, `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or their legacy `product-marketing-context.md` equivalents; reconcile conflicts with current evidence instead of merging stale facts silently.

Identify audience/buying situation, traffic source, page purpose, existing alternative, deliverable/offer, available proof, principal objections, and primary action. Define success as a qualified enquiry, completed order, activation, or other agreed outcome; keep clicks separate. Record unknowns that affect claims or scope. Ask only when a missing fact prevents correct work; otherwise proceed with labeled planning assumptions.

Respect the user's existing brand, reference direction, language, stack, scope, and design rules. In Bray's projects, read `website-house-rules` if available before website edits; use existing approved design/hero references. Research advice does not authorize new features, fabricated content, deployments, outreach, or changes to other skills.

## Do the requested work

**Write/edit:** choose a main promise appropriate to the audience's knowledge, pair it with real proof, answer decision-critical objections, and specify what happens after the action. For strategy or a new campaign, explore a few distinct arguments before wording variants. For a small edit, make the edit directly. Keep features that explain value or substantiate a claim.

**Plan/design:** map the reader's questions and the information required to answer them. Identify business subtype, page job and visitor stage separately; a restaurant visit, catering quote, freight enquiry, parcel tracking, SaaS trial and enterprise evaluation need different paths. Select structure by intent, not a universal hero template. Give each section a decision purpose and omit filler. Use prose for connected explanation, bullets for parallel facts, tables for comparisons, and steps for sequences. Give relevant evidence and primary actions appropriate emphasis. Preserve helpful navigation and secondary research paths when the task requires them.

**Audit:** inspect the actual page and journey, on mobile and desktop when tools permit. Trace primary action to its destination. Rank issues by observed severity, affected users, evidence, and effort; distinguish broken behavior from hypotheses and taste. Recommend the smallest useful changes before a redesign.

**Implement, when requested:** reuse the current stack and components; prefer semantic HTML, native controls, and simple CSS. Check meaningful responsive states, keyboard/focus behavior, text resizing, errors, links, and the complete action path. Use existing functional and performance checks. Take fresh screenshots for visual changes when tools are available; otherwise state what remains unverified. Do not infer field performance from a single lab score.

## Evidence discipline

- Never invent testimonials, customers, statistics, scarcity, guarantees, product capabilities, prices, or study details. Use verified facts, omit the claim, or mark a content gap outside publishable copy.
- Before labeling copy publishable, check every named service/use case, included feature, operational commitment and material condition against the brief—not just numbers and testimonials. Plausible adjacent offers (such as recurring orders) still need support.
- Separate randomized experiments, observational cases, qualitative usability research, standards, and practitioner conventions. Record outcome, population, timeframe and denominator where disclosed. Missing details stay missing.
- Report absolute versus relative changes correctly. Do not transfer another company's uplift to the current page or promise a conversion increase.
- Treat font, color, fold placement, copy length and CTA count as context-dependent. Accessibility requirements are constraints, not experiments to optimize away.
- For low traffic, prioritize broken journeys, customer interviews and task checks over underpowered A/B tests. For experiments, use the reference's validity checks and keep downstream quality visible.

## Deliver

Lead with the requested copy, edit, plan, or implemented result. Scale explanation to the task. When relevant, include the facts used, critical gaps, why the structure fits the reader, and one practical validation step. For audits, show priority, observed evidence, proposed change, and success measure. Report what was checked and what remains uncertain. Do not create a pile of process files unless requested.
